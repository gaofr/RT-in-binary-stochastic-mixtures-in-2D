This is data repository for the paper "Efficient algorithms for accurately simulating radiative transfer in binary stochastic mixtures in two dimensions". 
The notations correspond to figures included in the paper.A brief description is the following:

a) In the directory of "fig6", it is the output of the radiative transfer for an atomic-mixing binary mixture. The first column is the time in units of 
picsecond (ps), and the second to fourth columns represent the radiation reflection at x=0, radiation transmission at x=Lx, and material energy density at 
x=L, which are normalized by corresponding values at t=0.

b) In the directory of "fig7", it is data for the time evolution of the reflection and transmission of radiation at x=Lx for various grid sizes. For each 
case, the first column is the time in units of picsecond (ps), and the second and third columns are the radiation reflection at x=0 and radiation
transmission at x=Lx, which are in units of J/(cm^2ps).

c) In the directory of "fig8", it is data for the ensemble-averaged radiation reflection and transmission fluxes over 100 physical realizations. The first 
column is the time in units of picsecond (ps). The second column is the ensemble-averaged radiation reflection at x=0 [in units of J/(cm^2ps)] with its
standard deviation in the third column. The fourth column is the ensemble-averaged radiation transmission at x=Lx [in units of J/(cm^2ps)] with its standard 
deviation in the fifth column.

d) In the directory of "fig10", it is data for the ensemble-averaged transmission probability at Lx for different quadrature ordinates. The first column is
the distance tranversed by the light, the second column is the transmission probability with its standard deviation in the third column.

e) In the directory of "fig11", it is the data for the ensemble-averaged transmission of radiation at Lx for five particle size distributions. For each case,
the first column is the time in units of picsecond (ps). The second column is the ensemble-averaged radiation transmission at x=L [in units of J/(cm^2ps)]
with its standard deviation in the third column. The fourth column is the ensemble-averaged material energy density at x=Lx [in units of J/(cm^3)] with its 
standard deviation in the fifth column.

f) In the directory of "fig12", it is data for the transmission of radiation at Lx. For each case, the first column is the time in units of picsecond (ps),
and the second and third columns are the radiation reflection at x=0 and radiation transmission at x=Lx, which are in units of J/(cm^2ps).
